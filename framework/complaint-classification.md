# Complaint Classification Framework

## Purpose

The purpose of this classification framework is to ensure that customer complaints are analysed consistently, objectively, and in a way that supports meaningful insight generation.

Rather than treating complaints as isolated incidents, this framework structures each case using a standard set of dimensions. This enables reliable aggregation, comparison, and root cause analysis across large complaint volumes.

---

## Core Classification Dimensions

Each complaint is reviewed and categorised across the following dimensions.

---

### 1. Complaint Trigger

Identifies the primary event or process stage that led to the complaint.

Examples include:
- Policy renewal or auto-renewal
- Policy documentation delivery
- Medical disclosure or assessment
- Payment or financial transaction
- Policy cancellation or cooling-off period

This dimension helps identify high-risk points within the customer journey.

---

### 2. Customer Awareness & Understanding

Assesses whether the customer was reasonably aware of the process or outcome that led to the complaint.

Classification indicators:
- Customer fully aware and understood the process
- Customer partially aware or misunderstood key details
- Customer not aware of the process or outcome

This dimension is critical in identifying communication gaps versus operational failures.

---

### 3. Root Cause Category

Classifies the underlying driver of the complaint.

Common root cause categories:
- Communication clarity or timing
- Process or system limitation
- Policy or regulatory constraint
- Agent behaviour or decision-making
- Customer assumption or expectation mismatch

This ensures complaints are not misclassified based on surface-level reasons.

---

### 4. Preventability Indicator

Determines whether the complaint could have been reasonably prevented through changes in process, communication, or behaviour.

Values:
- Preventable
- Partially preventable
- Not preventable

This dimension supports prioritisation of improvement actions.

---

### 5. Impact Type

Assesses the nature of the impact experienced by the customer.

Examples:
- Financial impact
- Emotional distress or frustration
- Time or effort required to resolve
- Loss of trust or confidence

Understanding impact type helps organisations assess complaint severity beyond volume alone.

---

## Classification Principles

To maintain analytical integrity, the following principles apply:

- Complaints are classified based on evidence, not customer sentiment alone  
- Multiple dimensions may apply, but a single primary trigger is identified  
- Classification decisions should be auditable and repeatable  
- Ambiguous cases are documented with rationale  

---

## Output of Classification

The structured classification enables:
- Consistent complaint analysis across analysts  
- Reliable aggregation for trend and pattern detection  
- Clear linkage between complaints and decision-making  
- Improved accuracy in root cause reporting  

This framework serves as the foundation for root cause analysis and insight aggregation stages.
