# Decision Mapping Framework

## Purpose

The purpose of the decision mapping framework is to ensure that analytical insights derived from complaint data lead to clear, accountable, and measurable actions.

Rather than producing insights in isolation, this framework explicitly links root causes to decision points, ownership, and expected outcomes.

---

## From Insight to Action

Each validated insight is assessed against three key questions:

1. What decision does this insight inform?
2. Who is accountable for that decision?
3. What change is expected as a result?

This ensures insights are actionable and not purely informational.

---

## Decision Mapping Structure

Insights are mapped using the following structure.

---

### 1. Insight Summary

A concise, evidence-based description of the identified issue.

Example:
> A high proportion of renewal-related complaints are driven by customers being unaware of automated payment timing, despite receiving documentation.

---

### 2. Root Cause Reference

Links the insight back to one or more validated root causes, such as:
- Communication timing or clarity
- Process design
- Behavioural triggers
- System limitations

This ensures decisions address causes, not symptoms.

---

### 3. Decision Options

Defines the possible actions available to the organisation.

Examples:
- Change timing or format of customer communications
- Introduce proactive customer contact at high-risk points
- Update agent guidance or escalation thresholds
- Adjust system prompts or alerts

Multiple options may exist, depending on feasibility and risk.

---

### 4. Decision Ownership

Identifies the team or role responsible for making and implementing the decision.

Examples:
- Operations leadership
- Customer experience teams
- Compliance or policy owners
- Technology or systems teams

Clear ownership supports accountability and follow-through.

---

### 5. Expected Outcome

Defines the intended impact of the decision.

Examples:
- Reduction in preventable complaints
- Lower escalation volumes
- Improved customer understanding
- Increased trust and satisfaction

Outcomes are framed in measurable terms where possible.

---

## Prioritisation Considerations

Not all insights require immediate action. Decisions are prioritised based on:
- Complaint volume and recurrence
- Customer impact severity
- Preventability
- Regulatory or compliance risk
- Implementation effort

This supports balanced, risk-aware decision-making.

---

## Feedback Loop

Post-implementation outcomes are monitored to assess effectiveness.

This includes:
- Tracking complaint volumes post-change
- Reviewing customer feedback trends
- Reassessing root causes where issues persist

Insights are refined continuously, creating a closed feedback loop between analysis and decision-making.

---

## Value of Decision Mapping

By explicitly linking insights to decisions, this framework:
- Prevents insight stagnation
- Supports evidence-based leadership decisions
- Improves accountability across teams
- Drives sustained service improvement  

Decision mapping is the final step that transforms complaint analysis into organisational value.
