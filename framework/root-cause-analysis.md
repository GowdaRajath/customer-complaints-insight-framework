# Root Cause Analysis Framework

## Objective

The objective of this framework is to identify the true underlying drivers of customer complaints, rather than relying on surface-level or stated reasons alone.

Root cause analysis enables organisations to move from reactive complaint resolution to proactive prevention by addressing systemic issues that generate repeated dissatisfaction.

---

## Surface Reasons vs Root Causes

Customer complaints are often recorded using high-level reasons such as:
- “Customer unhappy”
- “Amount taken without approval”
- “Not aware of policy”
- “Documents not received”

While valid, these descriptions rarely explain *why* the issue occurred.

This framework focuses on uncovering the deeper drivers behind these outcomes.

---

## Root Cause Analysis Dimensions

Each complaint is assessed across the following analytical lenses.

---

### 1. Process-Related Causes

Identifies whether the complaint originated from a process design or execution issue.

Examples:
- Timing of automated actions (e.g. renewal payments)
- Gaps between process steps
- Dependencies between systems or teams
- Lack of proactive checkpoints

This helps distinguish individual errors from systemic weaknesses.

---

### 2. Communication-Related Causes

Assesses whether communication clarity, timing, or channel contributed to the complaint.

Indicators include:
- Information provided but not clearly understood
- Communication sent but not received or noticed
- Over-reliance on written communication for complex processes
- Mismatch between customer expectations and messaging

Communication-related causes are often highly preventable.

---

### 3. Behavioural Causes

Examines the role of human behaviour in the complaint outcome.

Examples:
- Agent decision-making or ownership
- Inconsistent explanations
- Escalation triggered by customer request rather than necessity
- Customer assumptions or prior experiences influencing expectations

This dimension supports targeted coaching and guidance.

---

### 4. Policy or Regulatory Constraints

Identifies complaints driven by non-negotiable rules or compliance requirements.

Examples:
- Cooling-off period regulations
- Mandatory processing timelines
- Legal or underwriting constraints

This helps distinguish complaints that require explanation improvements rather than process changes.

---

### 5. System or Tool Limitations

Assesses whether system design or technical limitations contributed to the issue.

Examples:
- Limited system prompts or alerts
- Manual workarounds increasing error risk
- Poor visibility of customer history or status
- Delayed data updates across platforms

These insights often inform technology or tooling improvements.

---

## Root Cause Validation

To ensure analytical accuracy:
- Root causes are supported by evidence from case notes or data fields  
- Assumptions are explicitly documented  
- Where multiple causes exist, a primary driver is identified  
- Edge cases are reviewed to avoid misclassification  

This validation ensures insights remain credible and defensible.

---

## Output of Root Cause Analysis

Effective root cause analysis enables:
- Identification of high-impact improvement opportunities  
- Reduction in repeat and avoidable complaints  
- Clear prioritisation of corrective actions  
- Stronger linkage between insight and decision-making  

This framework directly feeds into insight aggregation and decision mapping stages.
