# Insights Summary (Sample Application)

## Purpose

This document provides an example of how the Customer Complaints Insight Framework can be applied to structured complaint data in order to generate actionable insights.

The insights presented here are illustrative and are based on a synthetic dataset included in this repository.

---

## Dataset Overview

The sample dataset contains complaint records across key process stages, including:
- Auto-renewal and payment processing
- Policy documentation delivery
- Medical disclosure and assessment
- Policy cancellation and customer support interactions

Each record is classified using the framework dimensions outlined in the classification and root cause documentation.

---

## Key Insights Identified

### Insight 1: Auto-Renewal Complaints Driven by Awareness Gaps

A significant proportion of auto-renewal-related complaints are associated with customers being unaware of automated payment timing.

Although documentation may have been issued, the timing and format of communication appear insufficient to ensure customer understanding.

**Root Causes Identified:**
- Communication timing
- Customer assumption
- Over-reliance on written notifications

**Decision Implications:**
- Review renewal communication strategy
- Introduce proactive reminders at high-risk points
- Simplify messaging around automated payments

---

### Insight 2: Preventable Complaints Linked to Communication Channels

Complaints related to documentation delivery frequently involve customers who did not receive or notice email communications.

This suggests that channel reliance may be contributing to preventable dissatisfaction.

**Root Causes Identified:**
- Communication channel limitations
- Lack of delivery confirmation
- Customer inbox management issues

**Decision Implications:**
- Review multi-channel communication options
- Introduce confirmation or follow-up mechanisms
- Improve clarity of subject lines and messaging

---

### Insight 3: Escalation Does Not Always Correlate with Severity

Some escalated complaints show lower objective impact but higher emotional response, often driven by misunderstanding rather than operational failure.

**Root Causes Identified:**
- Behavioural factors
- Expectation mismatch
- Escalation triggered by customer request

**Decision Implications:**
- Strengthen agent guidance on escalation thresholds
- Support agents with clearer resolution pathways
- Reduce unnecessary escalations through earlier clarification

---

## Cross-Cutting Observations

Across multiple complaint categories, the following themes emerge:
- A high proportion of complaints are preventable
- Communication clarity and timing are recurring drivers
- Escalation risk is influenced by perception as much as process

These themes highlight the value of analysing complaints as a collective signal rather than isolated cases.

---

## Value of the Framework

Applying this framework enables organisations to:
- Prioritise improvements based on evidence
- Reduce avoidable complaint volumes
- Improve customer understanding and trust
- Support accountable, data-driven decisions

This example demonstrates how structured complaint analysis can move organisations from reactive resolution to proactive service improvement.
